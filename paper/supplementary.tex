\section{Overall-Time Handling in the 2014 and 2026 Releases} \label{app:2014}
Version~3.0 intentionally differs from the 2014 application in the organ-at-risk calculation. The legacy source is archived at \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16739883}; the relevant block is \texttt{pushbutton4\_Callback} in \texttt{eqd\_matlb.m}, lines 3186--3245. Captured outputs are distributed in \texttt{tests/data/golden.jsonl}, allowing the comparison to be reproduced without \texttt{MATLAB}.

\subsection*{Common Notation}
A course delivers $n$ fractions of dose $d$, and $d_r$ denotes the reference fraction size. Its per-fraction contribution is 
    \begin{equation}
    L(d)=
    \begin{cases}
    d\left[1+\dfrac{(1+H_m)d}{\alpha/\beta}\right],
      & d<d_t,\\[1ex]
    d_t\left(1+\dfrac{d_t}{\alpha/\beta}\right)
      +\dfrac{\gamma}{\alpha}(d-d_t),
      & d\geq d_t,
    \end{cases}
    \qquad d_t=2\,\alpha/\beta .
    \label{eq:response}
    \end{equation}
Here $H_m$ is the incomplete-repair term. Consistently with the original model, two fractions per day are supported only on the low-dose branch; hence $H_m=0$
for $d\geq d_t$. A twice-daily course is therefore admissible only when $d$ is below the transition doses of both selected tissues. For five treatment days per week, $s$ treatment sessions span
    \begin{equation}
    \Theta(s) \;=\;
    \begin{cases}
    s, & s < 6,\\[0.6ex]
    s + 2\left(\left\lfloor \dfrac{s-6}{5} \right\rfloor + 1\right), & s \geq 6,
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:calendar}
    \end{equation}
calendar days, weekends included. The cumulative proliferation terms are
    \begin{equation}
    P_\mathrm{target}(T) = D_\mathrm{prol}\,(T - T_k)_{+},    \qquad    P_\mathrm{organ}(T) = D_\mathrm{prol}\,T ,    \qquad    D_\mathrm{prol} = \frac{\ln 2}{\alpha\,T_p},
    \label{eq:loss}
    \end{equation}
with a kick-off time for the target and no kick-off time for the organ at risk \cite{vandyk1989}. The \texttt{BED} delivered over $[t_0,t_1]$ is
    \begin{equation}
    \mathrm{BED}(d, n; t_0, t_1) \;=\; n\,L(d) - \big[P(t_1) - P(t_0)\big],
    \label{eq:bed}
    \end{equation}
and the reference fraction count $n_r$ solves
    \begin{equation}
    \mathrm{BED}\big(d_r,\,n_r;\,\tau,\,\tau + \Theta(n_r)\big)     \;=\; \mathrm{BED}\big(d,\,n;\,t_0,\,t_1\big),    \qquad    \mathrm{EQD}_{d_r} = n_r\,d_r ,
    \label{eq:core}
    \end{equation}
where $\tau$ is the reference time already spent by preceding courses. Successive-course additivity follows because increments of $P$ telescope.

\subsection*{Source-Level Difference}
For the organ at risk, the 2014 routine first searches a grid of hundredths of a fraction:
    \begin{equation}
    n_r \;=\; \arg\min_{x} \left|\;
    \Big[n L(d)   - \tfrac{7}{5}\,n\,D_\mathrm{prol}\Big]
    -\Big[x L(d_r) - \tfrac{7}{5}\,x\,D_\mathrm{prol}\Big] \right| ,
    \label{eq:legacy-search}
    \end{equation}
It then reports
    \begin{equation}
    \mathrm{EQD}^{\,2014}_{d_r} \;=\; n_r\,d_r
    \;-\;\big[\Theta(n + g) - \Theta(n_r)\big]\,D_\mathrm{prol} .
    \label{eq:legacy-report}
    \end{equation}
Thus, overall time is already present on both sides of Eq.~\eqref{eq:legacy-search} and is applied a second time in Eq.~\eqref{eq:legacy-report}. Version~3.0 instead evaluates Eqs.~\eqref{eq:calendar}--\eqref{eq:core} directly: one calendar is used on both sides, and nothing is added after $n_r d_r$. The distinction can be tested by a round trip: under a given calendar convention, the reported reference schedule must reproduce the \texttt{BED} it replaces. For a rectum
($\alpha/\beta=3.9$\,Gy, $D_\mathrm{prol}=0.3$\,Gy\,d$^{-1}$) receiving $20\times3$\,Gy, each column below uses the calendar convention of the corresponding version:
    \begin{center}
    \small
    \begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
    \toprule
     & \textbf{2014} & \textbf{version 3.0} \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{BED} of the evaluated schedule (Gy)  & 97.754  & 98.354 \\
    Equivalent dose reported (Gy)                 & 82.687  & 75.251 \\
    \texttt{BED} of the reference schedule (Gy)  & \textbf{107.727} & \textbf{98.354} \\
    Round-trip residual (Gy)                       & $+9.973$ & $0.000$ \\
    \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
The 2014 value fails the round trip because the time difference is charged twice; the version~3.0 value closes by construction. The replacement of the long-run approximation $7n/5$ by the exact staircase is a separate convention: using $7n/5$ consistently would give 75.03\,Gy rather than 75.25\,Gy in this case. Legacy outputs remain reproducible through \texttt{Options.legacy\_2014()}; this option is exercised by the 4438-schedule non-regression suite and is not used by the current calculation.

\subsection*{Target-Volume Results}
The closest agreement is \texttt{HYPO-RT-PC} ($-0.06$\,Gy). The two \texttt{CHHiP} arms are also ordered correctly: the arm that met non-inferiority lies above the arm for which non-inferiority was not demonstrated. These observations support qualitative coherence, but not calibration. \texttt{PROFIT} is the clearest counterexample: the model places 60\,Gy in 20 fractions 5.54\,Gy below 78\,Gy in 39 fractions although the trial demonstrated non-inferiority. The result is highly sensitive to $\alpha/\beta$. Under the plain \texttt{LQ} model with $\alpha/\beta=1.5$\,Gy,
    \[
    \mathrm{EQD2}=60\,\frac{3+1.5}{2+1.5}=77.14\ \mathrm{Gy},
    \]
which is within 1\,Gy of the conventional arm. This is consistent with the pooled clinical estimate $\alpha/\beta=1.4$\,Gy (0.9--2.2) reported for prostate cancer
\cite{miralbell2012alphabeta}. A fixed value of 3.1\,Gy is therefore not universally representative.

\subsection*{Spinal-Cord Results}
For 25.3\,Gy in five fractions, the calculated 1.45\,\% \texttt{NTCP} lies within the 1--5\,\% \texttt{HyTEC} range. This numerical agreement is weak evidence because \texttt{HyTEC} constrains a maximum dose to the cord or thecal sac, whereas the software contains no spatial or volume information. The conventional case is more informative: 50\,Gy in 25 fractions gives 5.13\,\% \texttt{NTCP}, compared with approximately 0.2\,\% in \texttt{QUANTEC}. The discrepancy concerns the historical \texttt{Lyman} parameters, not the equivalent-dose calculation, and is consistent with the reported failure of historical Lyman--Kutcher--Burman parameters in spinal-cord radiosurgery \cite{daly2012lkb}.

\subsection*{Interpretation}
These comparisons support qualitative coherence for several schedules, but they do not validate clinical outcomes. They also identify the two dominant limits of
the distributed parameter set: use of a single prostate $\alpha/\beta$ value and poor calibration of the spinal-cord complication curve. Consequently,  \texttt{NTCP} and \texttt{TCP} outputs must remain indicative, and the software must not be described as clinically validated.
